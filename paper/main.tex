\begin{center}
  {\Large\bfseries Abstract}
\end{center}

\begin{quote}
Mechanistic interpretability often recovers circuits by many causal
interventions. We ask whether a small subgraph of the \emph{live} attention
routing graph---built from one forward pass---already isolates causally
relevant structure on known tasks. Nodes are token positions; edges are
attention routes \((\ell,h,j\!\to\!i)\) scored by attention \(\times\) value
norm. A short backward top-\(k\) walk from the answer position yields candidate
edges \(S\); we verify by zeroing those weights (with renormalization) and
comparing the task-metric drop to size-matched random edges. On induction
heads, GPT-2 Small yields mean gap \(+3.55\)\,nats (\(n{=}23\)); GPT-2 Medium
and Pythia-410M reproduce the sign (\({+}1.85\), \({+}1.78\)). The same
protocol transfers to IOI on GPT-2 Small (gap \({+}5.17\)). Live extract uses
one forward versus \(\sim\)145 for a head-patch sweep
(\(\sim\)106\(\times\) wall-clock). Gaps remain positive across templates,
moderate lengths, score variants, and \(k\!\ge\!10\); late-only layers fail.
Failures are rare (\(\sim\)5\%) and concentrated on larger models; recovered
sets overlap known heads incompletely. We claim a cheap routing sketch with
causal support---not completeness or feature semantics. We release the
implementation and evaluation artifacts.
\end{quote}

\section{Introduction}

Attention implements communication channels between positions
\cite{vaswani2017,elhage2021}.
On narrow tasks such as induction \cite{olsson2022} and indirect object
identification (IOI) \cite{wang2023}, those channels form sparse circuits that
heavier methods---activation patching, ACDC, attribution graphs---can recover
at substantial cost \cite{conmy2023,heimersheim2024,ferrando2024,ameisen2025}.

The scientific risk is to treat every high-attention edge as mechanism, or
every mechanistic claim as requiring a full offline search.
Numerous efforts have since automated circuit discovery, while also improving
attribution and feature-level graphs
\cite{conmy2023,ferrando2024,marks2025,syed2023}.
In most such settings, however, the candidate set is built by many
interventions or by a separately trained dictionary, not by reading the
attention routing graph of a single forward as a first-class object.

In this work we propose extracting a live circuit sketch directly from that
routing graph. Nodes are token positions; edges are attention routes
\((\ell,h,j\!\to\!i)\) with head identity preserved. Default score is
attention \(\times\) value norm. A short top-\(k\) walk of depth \(B\) from
the answer token yields \(S\); identity of the sketch is this extract rule, not
a verbal head name. We verify with edge ablation versus size-matched random
controls. On GPT-2 Small / Medium and Pythia-410M, induction gaps are positive;
the same protocol transfers to IOI on GPT-2 Small; live extract is roughly two
orders of magnitude cheaper in forwards than a head-patch sweep.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Routing graph}
Fix a prompt with \(T\) positions and root \(t^\star\) (answer / last token).
One forward yields attention \(A[\ell,h,i,j]\) and values at keys.
An edge \((\ell,h,j\!\to\!i)\) means head \(h\) at layer \(\ell\) routes from
key \(j\) to query \(i\). We keep all layers and heads, ban BOS keys, and
exclude self-edges. Default score:
\[
s(\ell,h,j\!\to\!i)
=
A[\ell,h,i,j]\,\bigl\|V[\ell,h,j]\bigr\|_2 .
\]
Raw attention is a secondary score. Identity is this weight of routing, not a
learned dictionary atom.

\subsection{Extract}
Let the frontier be \(\{t^\star\}\). For hop \(1..B\), take the top-\(k\)
incoming scored edges to each frontier query, add them to \(S\), and expand to
their key positions. Defaults: \(k{=}15\), \(B{=}4\). Threshold \(\tau\) is an
alternative cut (same walk, score floor).

\subsection{Verify}
Metric \(m\): target-token logit (induction) or
\(\mathrm{logit}(\mathrm{IO})-\mathrm{logit}(\mathrm{S})\) (IOI).
Ablate by zeroing attention on edges in \(S\) and renormalizing over remaining
keys. Control: sample \(|S|\) valid causal edges uniformly; repeat \(R\) draws;
report mean drop.
\[
\Delta_S = m_{\mathrm{full}} - m_{\mathrm{ablate}(S)},
\qquad
\mathrm{gap} = \Delta_S - \mathbb{E}[\Delta_R] .
\]
Success: \(\mathrm{gap}>0\) (extracted edges hurt the behavior more than
random).

\subsection{Pass criteria}
An example is accepted when \(\Delta_S > \Delta_R\). A model--task cell is
reported by mean \(\Delta_S\), mean \(\Delta_R\), gap, and fail count
(\(\Delta_S\le\Delta_R\)). Cost compares live extract (1 forward) and
live+verify (\(1+1+R\) forwards with \(R\) random ablations) to a full
head-patch sweep (clean run + one forward per head).

\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
Models: GPT-2 Small (primary), GPT-2 Medium, Pythia-410M
\cite{biderman2023}. Tasks: induction templates
\texttt{The A B. The A}$\,\to\,$\texttt{B} with single-token pairs
(\(n{=}23\)); IOI dual-name templates on GPT-2 Small (\(n{=}23\)).
Protocol: score \(\mathrm{attn}\times\|v\|\), \(k{=}15\), \(B{=}4\), BOS banned,
\(R{=}10\), seed \(0\). Attention maps are read from a standard
transformer hook library \cite{nanda2022}.
Artifacts for each run record seeds, prompts, thresholds, and metrics.

\subsection{Causal gap on induction and IOI}
Table~\ref{tab:main} summarizes mean drops. On GPT-2 Small induction,
\(\Delta_S{=}{+}3.66\) vs.\ \(\Delta_R{=}{+}0.11\) (gap \({+}3.55\)); every
example beats random (\(0/23\) fails). Medium and Pythia reproduce the sign
with smaller absolute gaps. IOI on Small is not a one-task artifact: gap
\({+}5.17\), again \(0/23\) fails.

\subsection{Cost vs.\ head-patching}
On five Small induction prompts (CPU), live extract averages \(0.056\)\,s /
1~forward; live+verify (\(R{=}10\)) \(0.48\)\,s / 12~forwards; full head-patch
\(5.94\)\,s / 145~forwards (\(\approx\)106\(\times\) wall-clock, \(145\times\)
forwards vs.\ extract alone). Edge-ACDC is strictly more expensive; the head
sweep is a lower bound on patching cost.

\subsection{Structure recovery is partial}
On Small induction, extracted sets consistently include known previous-token /
induction heads (L4H11, L5H5, L5H8 in nearly all \(23\) examples) but sparsely
hit later named heads (e.g.\ L6H9 in \(3/23\); L6H10 in \(0/23\)). Causal
success therefore indicates overlap with a sufficient path, not complete
textbook recovery.

\subsection{Failure cases}
Across \(92\) induction+IOI example-runs, \(5\) causal failures
(\(\sim\)5\%), all on Medium/Pythia induction (ablation helps the target or
falls below random). Small induction and IOI are clean. Residual modes:
distractor high-score routes, incomplete multi-hop coverage under fixed \(B\),
and attention mass that is not causation (mitigated by BOS ban and
\(\mathrm{attn}\times\|v\|\)).

\subsection{Ablations}
All on GPT-2 Small induction (\(n{\le}10\) unless noted).

\paragraph{Top-\(k\) / \(\tau\).}
\(k{=}5\) under-extracts (gap \({+}0.04\), \(4/10\) fails); \(k{\ge}10\) is
solid; default \(k{=}15\) has \(0\) fails (gap \({+}3.93\) on this slice).
\(\tau{=}5\) over-prunes (gap flips negative); moderate
\(\tau\in[0.5,2]\) remains positive.

\paragraph{Layer bands.}
All layers \(\approx\) early (\(0\)--\(3\)) \(\gg\) mid (\(4\)--\(7\)); late
(\(8\)--\(11\)) fails (gap \({-}0.19\), \(6/10\) fails). Restricting to the
textbook induction band alone under-extracts causal mass relative to all-layer
routing.

\paragraph{Scores.}
Raw attention gap \({+}3.21\); \(\mathrm{attn}\times\|v\|\) gap \({+}3.93\)
(both \(0\) fails). We keep \(\mathrm{attn}\times\|v\|\) as default.

\paragraph{Template / length.}
Gaps stay positive on \texttt{the\_dot}, \texttt{bare}, \texttt{then},
\texttt{yesterday} (worst \({+}2.19\)) and with \(0\)--\(4\) filler sentences
before the pattern (worst \({+}2.57\)). One fail each on \texttt{bare} and
one-filler; not a single-template artifact.

\section{Discussion}

The measurement stack is the durable part of this work. Size-matched random
ablation separates routing extract from attention mass alone under a fixed
\(|S|\) budget. Cost is honest: one forward for the sketch, a small constant
for verify, versus a lower-bound head sweep. Partial head recovery is reported
rather than claimed as complete circuit identity.

Novelty is thinner than a full replacement for ACDC or attribution graphs
\cite{conmy2023,ferrando2024,ameisen2025,marks2025}. We do not claim
human-readable feature names, and we do not claim to replace sparse feature
circuits for concept discovery. The wedge is routing-first extract, a negative
result about late-only layers and over-tight \(\tau\), template/length
robustness, and a reproducible verify suite on known tasks.

Scope stays sharp. Verification uses attention zero+renorm, not path patching.
All primary numbers are for GPT-2 Small / Medium and Pythia-410M on synthetic
induction and IOI prompts.

\section{Limitations}

This study uses small open models and two known tasks; results may not transfer
to frontier models or open-ended behavior. Extracted \(S\) mixes true circuit
edges with high-score distractors; we do not claim necessity or monosemanticity
of every edge. \(|S|\) grows with prompt length; fixed \(k,B\) can miss deeper
composition. We do not train SAEs or explain feature semantics.

\section{Conclusion}

We extract a live attention routing subgraph \(S\) from one forward and score
it with edge ablation versus size-matched random controls. On induction, GPT-2
Small / Medium and Pythia-410M show positive gaps; on IOI, GPT-2 Small gap is
\({+}5.17\). Live extract is \(\sim\)145\(\times\) fewer forwards than a
head-patch sweep. Ablations support \(k{\ge}10\),
\(\mathrm{attn}\times\|v\|\), and all-layer routing; late-only layers fail.
We release the implementation and evaluation artifacts, with sketch identity
defined as the routing extract rule itself.
